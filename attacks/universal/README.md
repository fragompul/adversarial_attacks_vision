# Universal

Every attack in `whitebox/`, `blackbox/`, and `physical/` is optimized for one specific image. This folder asks a stranger question: does a single, fixed perturbation exist that fools a network on a large fraction of images it was never optimized against at all?

`01_UAP` implements Moosavi-Dezfooli et al.'s 2017 algorithm directly: walk repeatedly through a set of training images, and whenever the current universal perturbation does not already fool a given one, use DeepFool to find the minimal extra push needed and fold it into the running perturbation, projected back into an L-infinity ball after every update. Built from 30 MiniImageNet images against MobileNetV2, the perturbation reaches a 96.7% fooling rate on those same 30 images after 5 passes, and, more importantly, a 40% fooling rate on 20 completely different, held-out images it never saw during construction. That is the honest reading of a reduced-scale replication: the perturbation clearly overfits to its 30 training images (96.7% vs. 40%), but the fact that a fixed, image-independent noise pattern still fools 40% of unrelated natural images at all is itself the striking part of the original paper's claim, confirmed here at a much smaller scale than the thousands of ImageNet images the original work used.
